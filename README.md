1) Hospital Bed Management – Project (1)

In a bustling hospital where every day feels like a race against time, the flow of patients rises and falls like waves in a stormy sea. As the weeks pass, more patients walk through the doors seeking help, and the hospital stretches itself to meet their needs. Beds fill up quickly—often over 90%—leaving little room for emergencies. Some departments, like General Medicine and the Emergency Unit, are packed almost year-round, while specialized units like the ICU deliver exceptional care but face exhausted staff. Every admission, discharge, and request tells a part of the story: a story of growing demand, limited space, and a dedicated team trying to keep the system running smoothly. Amidst the rush, patient satisfaction rises and dips depending on how crowded the hospital becomes, showing that every bed impacts not just capacity but also the quality of care.
As the year unfolds, the imbalance between requests and available beds becomes more dramatic. Some weeks see turnaway rates soaring as high as 60–80%, forcing the hospital to redirect patients despite its best efforts. The length of stay for most patients is short, which helps free beds, but unpredictable spikes in long stays add to the pressure. Discharges and admissions move in a delicate dance—sometimes in sync, sometimes struggling to keep pace. Through these patterns, a larger narrative emerges: the hospital is doing everything it can, but demand is rapidly outgrowing capacity. The numbers reveal not just operational challenges, but the deeper story of a system fighting to stay resilient in the face of overwhelming need.
